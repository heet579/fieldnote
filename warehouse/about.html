Whose data is this?

The readings are published by City of Adelaide as Torrens Lake Meterological Data, under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. Fieldnote is an independent way to explore those public records.

The archive was downloaded on 11 November 2026. Its published file was last modified on 10 November 2025. Those are file dates, not the dates the water was measured.

This is a historical archive. It doesn't show current water conditions and shouldn't be used to decide whether the water is safe for swimming or drinking.

What's included?

We bring together 120 monthly files, covering June 2015 to September 2018. Temperature, electrical conductivity and dissolved oxygen are available at each of the three locations.

2015 and 2018 are partial years. Some dates are also absent within the archive. You can view one year at a time, explore all available years, or compare the three locations.

Conductivity uses the published E_Cond field, measured at the water temperature. It is different from the separately supplied, temperature-corrected Sp_Cond field. pH and depth-specific temperature fields aren't included in this view.

How should I read the chart?

Each reading gets the same weight.

The line shows monthly averages of the available readings. When locations are combined, a location with more readings contributes more to the average. The comparison switch lets you look at them separately.

A day with data isn't necessarily a complete day.

"Days with readings" counts dates with at least one valid measurement in your selection. It doesn't tell you whether every expected 15-minute reading arrived. A missing date might be a gap in the published files rather than a sensor outage.

The original clock has no timezone attached.

The source files don't state a UTC offset. Fieldnote keeps their reported dates and times rather than guessing a timezone or daylight-saving rule.

Data checks have limits.

__REJECTS__ missing numeric readings were set aside. Values haven't been filled in, interpolated or changed. Published zero values have been retained; passing a basic data check doesn't establish sensor calibration or scientific validity.

Take the data with you.

Daily summaries contain the average, number of original readings, lowest reading and highest reading for each day, location and measurement. The download button on the explorer gives you just your selected view.

How the records were brought together

We use the 120 monthly files inside the archive's torrens/ directory. The duplicate copies at the archive root are excluded. Three measurement fields are reshaped into individual readings, checked for numeric values and known units, and loaded into a local database.

The web page uses __DAILY__ daily summaries to keep it lightweight. Each summary carries its original sample count and extremes, so the monthly averages and lowest/highest values can be reconstructed without giving sparse days extra weight.

Every accepted reading has been compared with its source file. Reloading the same files added no duplicates. A separate recovery check verified that a restored database matched the original.
